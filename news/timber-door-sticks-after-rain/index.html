Why a timber door sticks after wet weather

A sticking door can be a clearance issue, but trimming is not always the first step.

By Ellis Services Group · Canberra carpentry advice
In brief

A sticking door can be a clearance issue, but trimming is not always the first step. The component and any moisture or support issue should be checked before the repair scope is agreed.

What changes after rain?

Timber absorbs and releases moisture. A door, frame or adjoining finish can move enough to affect the closing gap. Hinges and latch alignment can also contribute.

What should be checked?

Look for where the door touches the frame, whether the hinges are secure and whether water is reaching exposed edges. A carpenter can assess the door and frame before deciding on adjustment or repair.

When another trade may be needed

If the lock itself has failed, speak with a locksmith. If water is entering around the opening, the source should be addressed before the timber is finished.

What to send with your enquiry

Include a photo of the whole item, a close-up of the affected area, your Canberra suburb and any access details. If you are unsure whether the issue is carpentry work, describe what you see and we can discuss the next step.
