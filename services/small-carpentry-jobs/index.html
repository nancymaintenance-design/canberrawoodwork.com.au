What the work can include

Small carpentry jobs are best described as a list of distinct timber items rather than one vague request for a handyman. A loose shelf bracket, damaged trim, a sticking internal door and a short section of skirting may involve different materials and fixing methods even when they are all in one home. Group the items by room or outdoor area and note whether you want repair, replacement or advice on what can be retained. That makes the proposed visit and materials easier to discuss.

What homeowners commonly notice

Look for movement, splits, missing fixings, swelling, water marks and parts that no longer meet neatly. Note whether an issue appeared after flooring, painting, a leak or a change in use. A photo taken from across the room gives context; a close photo shows the joint or damaged edge. Please do not pull loose fittings further apart just to photograph them. Describe what happens during normal use and whether the item presents an immediate safety concern.

How the scope is assessed

Each item needs its own practical decision: can existing timber be refixed, does a matching piece need sourcing, or would replacement give a more durable result? We would consider the substrate behind a fixing, access for tools, the existing finish and any making good after removal. A long list can still be discussed together, but one hidden defect may change an item from a quick adjustment to a separately scoped repair. The agreed quote should identify included tasks rather than assume every small issue is identical.

Where this repair may stop

A small carpentry enquiry does not automatically cover plumbing, electrical work, pest treatment, glazing or structural changes. If a fitting is loose because the wall or supporting timber has deteriorated, the cause needs attention before a cosmetic refix is sensible. Painting, new hardware and disposal should also be identified in the scope. If a task proves larger than the original description, larger work can be assessed separately instead of being treated as an incidental extra.

Canberra conditions to mention

Canberra homes can have a mix of older timber details and newer manufactured boards. Matching a profile or finish is often more important than the apparent size of the repair. Seasonal movement can also affect tight doors and trim joints, so mention whether the problem is constant or comes and goes. For outside items, include the side of the house and whether rain or direct sun reaches the timber; that exposure can change material and finish choices.

Prepare your enquiry

Send a numbered list with one task per line, one wide photo and one close photo per item, your Canberra suburb, and the rooms or outside areas involved. Include approximate lengths or dimensions where practical and say if you already have replacement parts. Mention stairs, limited parking, pets, access hours or a tenancy handover date as planning details, without assuming a booking time. You can attach photos to the enquiry; further material can be shared later if requested.
