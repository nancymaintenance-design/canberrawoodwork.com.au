What the work can include

A new deck or extension is a separate project from replacing boards on an existing deck. The enquiry begins with the intended use, location, approximate footprint, height and relationship to the house, garden and boundaries. Material, stairs, balustrades, drainage and connections all affect what must be designed and checked. This page is for defining the questions and possible scope; it does not state that a particular deck is exempt from approval or that construction can begin from an online description.

What homeowners commonly notice

Show the proposed area from the house and garden, current ground levels, existing paving or structures, doors that would connect to the deck and any slope or drainage path. If extending an old deck, show its posts, framing and visible condition separately from the new area. Note overhead services, trees and tight side access. Photos help explain the site, but they cannot establish soil conditions, boundary positions or the capacity of an existing structure.

How the scope is assessed

A useful sequence starts with dimensions and intended loads, then design, supports, materials, access and relevant ACT approval or practitioner checks. New footing locations and deck set-out depend on ground levels, services, water flow, the proposed height and the relationship to existing doors; none can be confirmed from a rough online sketch alone. The condition of any deck being extended needs assessment before it is treated as a sound base. The proposed scope should distinguish design or documentation, construction, stairs and rails, finish, removal of existing material and coordination with other trades. A quotation should follow the required site and regulatory questions rather than assume a standard layout fits.

Where this repair may stop

Repairing an existing deck generally begins with the condition of retained components; a new build also needs the footprint, support layout and site constraints defined. New work and extensions may require approvals, engineering or licensed practitioners depending on their details. This page does not claim those permissions or a specific licence, and it does not replace advice from the relevant ACT authority or professional. Electrical fittings, drainage changes, excavation and retaining walls can introduce separate trades. A board-repair quote should not quietly become a deck-construction scope when support or footprint changes are involved.

Canberra conditions to mention

Canberra sites can combine hot sun, frost, sloping ground and different levels between indoor floors and gardens. Exposure and drainage affect material and finish choices; height and boundary proximity can affect design and approval questions. Mention where the proposed deck sits relative to the house, neighbouring property and runoff. The exact requirements are site-specific, so local context helps prepare the enquiry without serving as an approval determination.

Prepare your enquiry

Send a rough plan with approximate length, width and desired height, wide photos of the site and access route, and a description of how you expect to use the deck. Include existing plans or approval documents if available, any old deck involved, your Canberra suburb and known drainage or boundary constraints. You can attach photos to the enquiry; further material can be shared later if requested.
