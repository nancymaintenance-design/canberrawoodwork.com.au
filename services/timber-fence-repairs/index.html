What the work can include

Timber fence repairs may involve palings or pickets, rails, posts and the fixings that hold a section together. A missing board is different from a leaning run whose post has moved at ground level. Describe the length and position of the affected section, the fence style and whether you want a local repair or a discussion about replacing a larger run. Gates are worth identifying separately because their weight and movement place different demands on posts and hardware.

What homeowners commonly notice

Useful signs include loose palings, broken rails, a post that moves when the fence is pushed lightly, repeated sagging near a gate and timber that is soft at ground level. Show both sides if you can access them lawfully and safely. Note whether the lean is recent, worsening or present across a whole run. A photo can reveal alignment and damage, but it does not establish the condition below ground or identify who is responsible for a boundary fence.

How the scope is assessed

A repair discussion starts with posts and rails before cosmetic boards, because new palings cannot stabilise a failing support. Post and rail repair may require access on both sides, temporary removal of palings or a clear approach to the post base. Measure the damaged length and approximate fence height; note whether an existing profile or spacing needs matching. Check how new timber will meet the retained run, especially where its line already varies. Removal, disposal, access from one or both sides and adjacent paving or planting can all affect the work. The scope should make clear whether it includes isolated boards, a rail, a post or a whole section.

Where this repair may stop

Boundary position and owner agreement need to be settled by the owners or relevant authorities; a timber repair cannot determine either issue. Shared costs and local fencing rules are also not assumptions in a carpentry quote. If the intended repair changes the fence line, height or style, establish who can authorise that decision before materials are selected. Masonry walls, metal panels and electrical gate equipment require other expertise. If a fence is retaining soil or acting as a safety barrier, that function must be assessed separately. A leaning fence should not be described as a simple paling replacement until its supports are understood.

Canberra conditions to mention

Canberra fences can have very different exposure along the same boundary: sun and wind on one side, irrigation or shade on the other. Ground contact and water held near a post base often matter more than surface colour. Mention any nearby sprinkler, drainage path or soil build-up. In established suburbs, access may be limited by gardens and shared boundaries, so the route for materials and the neighbour-side access need early discussion.

Prepare your enquiry

Send a straight-on image of the whole affected run and close views of posts, rails and broken boards. Provide approximate fence height, damaged length, your Canberra suburb and whether the fence borders another property or a public area. Explain if a gate is attached and whether access is available on both sides. You can attach photos to the enquiry; further material can be shared later if requested.
