What the work can include

Timber weatherboard repairs concern an existing exterior cladding run, usually a damaged board or a local group of boards. Profiles, laps, corner trims and the junctions around openings all influence whether a section can be replaced neatly. The scope may include removing a failed board, checking exposed backing, fitting a compatible piece and preparing its finish. A single cracked board is a different enquiry from recurring moisture across a wall, so describe the extent as accurately as you can.

What homeowners commonly notice

Look for split ends, cupping, loose fixings, peeling paint at joints, soft timber and staining that returns after rain. Photograph the whole elevation as well as the affected boards so roof edges, windows, ground level and drainage are visible. Note whether the damage is concentrated under a gutter or window. Do not remove boards to investigate hidden framing yourself; the visible surface may not indicate what lies behind the cladding.

How the scope is assessed

Assess the board profile and lap so replacement fits adjacent courses and continues to shed water. Check fixings and sound timber at the edges of the proposed repair. Removal may expose damaged building wrap, framing or a water path behind the cladding; that finding can expand the scope beyond a few boards. The proposal should identify which boards and trim are included, how an older profile will be matched, and whether priming and painting are included.

Where this repair may stop

Unknown cladding materials, widespread envelope failure, asbestos concerns, structural framing damage or a persistent leak require separate assessment by the appropriate practitioner. Replacing a board cannot resolve water entry from a failed window or roof detail. Where the repair affects a controlled building element, relevant ACT requirements must be checked before work is accepted. A photograph cannot establish that the wall cavity is dry or that all adjacent timber is sound.

Canberra conditions to mention

Sun-facing walls in Canberra may show coating failure earlier, while sheltered walls can hold moisture around laps and board ends. Older houses can have weatherboard sizes or profiles that differ from current stock. Mention the elevation, whether an eave protects it and whether recent rain reveals staining. These facts help discuss a suitable timber and finish, and whether local replacement will blend with the existing wall.

Prepare your enquiry

Send a wide image of the wall, close views of affected boards and corners, and photos showing nearby windows, roof edges or ground contact. Estimate the number and length of damaged boards, wall height, access below and whether painting is requested. Include your Canberra suburb and any known leak history. You can attach photos to the enquiry; further material can be shared later if requested.
