What the work can include

This service addresses fittings and local timber or board damage in existing cupboards, wardrobes and drawers. Common tasks include a loose hinge, a drawer runner that no longer travels properly, a damaged fixing point, a misaligned door or a kickboard that has come away. It is useful to distinguish a hardware adjustment from replacement of a panel or whole cabinet. Describe the item’s age and material if known, and whether several units have the same fault.

What homeowners commonly notice

Photograph the unit closed and open, the hinge or runner, any part number on the fitting and the timber or board around the screws. Note if the door catches another door, if a drawer tilts under normal load or if moisture has swollen a panel. A short description of what changed and when is useful. Do not force a stuck runner or remove a heavy door to create photos; that can cause further damage.

How the scope is assessed

The first question is whether the existing fitting can be adjusted and securely refixed or whether a compatible replacement is required. Hinge and runner compatibility depends on hardware type, overlay, dimensions, substrate condition and access inside the cabinet. Check part availability before assuming a replacement will fit the old mounting holes; some hardware may need a different fixing approach. A failed fixing in particleboard may need a different repair from a loose screw in solid timber. If multiple doors are out of line, the cabinet itself may be moving. The scope should identify parts, labour and any finish repair separately.

Where this repair may stop

A complete made-to-measure cabinet, major kitchen redesign, electrical appliance work or active plumbing leak is a separate scope. A matching hinge or runner may be unavailable, so exact hardware equivalence cannot be promised from a broad photo. If water continues to enter a cabinet, the source must be addressed before replacing swollen board. Heavy wall-mounted units may need support or substrate assessment before fittings are changed.

Canberra conditions to mention

Homes around Canberra contain a wide mix of older joinery and newer modular cabinetry, and replacement hardware is not always interchangeable. Temperature and humidity changes can expose tight clearances, but a recurring leak under a sink is a different issue. Mention the cabinet’s room and any recent renovation or appliance change. Those details help decide whether the enquiry is about a single fitting, a panel or a larger installation question.

Prepare your enquiry

Send one wide image of the cabinet, an open view showing hinges or runners, and close photos of damaged fixing points or labels. Include approximate door or drawer dimensions, the number of affected units, whether spare parts are available, your suburb and any access restrictions. Describe the fault and its effect during normal use. You can attach photos to the enquiry; further material can be shared later if requested.
