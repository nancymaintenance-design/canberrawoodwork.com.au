What the work can include

Rotten timber is a condition to investigate, not a complete repair specification. It may affect a window sill, door jamb, weatherboard end, deck board, fascia or another exposed component. The visible soft patch can be smaller than the damaged area beneath a joint or coating. This page is a starting point for identifying the actual component and likely water source, then deciding whether local removal and replacement are suitable for that particular part.

What homeowners commonly notice

Record softness, crumbling fibres, discolouration, swelling, a coating that repeatedly blisters or a joint that opens after wet weather. Note where water may arrive from: a gutter, ground contact, flashing, plumbing, a deck surface or an exposed end grain. These are clues rather than a remote diagnosis. Keep photographs broad enough to show the whole item and its surroundings as well as the affected patch. Avoid disturbing timber that may be supporting a load.

How the scope is assessed

Start by tracing the moisture source before replacement, whether water comes from a gutter, joint, ground contact or another component. Removal must reach sound timber at the repair joint; a surface photo cannot establish that boundary. Compatible replacement timber, fixings, exposure and finish all affect durability. Damage continuing behind cladding or into a support requires a broader opening-up and assessment scope. A filler or surface patch cannot substitute for checking the timber beneath, and the proposed work should separate repair of the component from correction of the cause.

Where this repair may stop

Active plumbing or roofing leaks, pest treatment and structural timber damage require the appropriate trade or practitioner. Mould, hidden cavity damage and unknown cladding materials may also need separate assessment. We cannot promise that a visible area defines the full extent of decay. If a load-bearing part is affected, the structural repair pathway and any ACT requirements must be checked before that work is accepted.

Canberra conditions to mention

Dry spells can make damaged timber look stable even where water has entered during storms. Seasonal changes and strong sun can also open exterior joints and wear coatings, especially at end grain. In Canberra homes, exposure varies sharply between a sheltered entry and a west-facing fascia or deck edge. Mention when the damage was first noticed and whether it follows rain; this history can make an enquiry more useful than the word “rot” alone.

Prepare your enquiry

Identify the component, your Canberra suburb and whether it is inside, under cover or fully exposed. Send a wide photo showing nearby guttering, ground, wall or opening where relevant, plus a close view of the affected timber. Include approximate length of the damaged area, height above ground and any known leak or pest report. You can attach photos to the enquiry; further material can be shared later if requested.
