What the work can include

Fascia and eaves work concerns the exposed timber edge below a roof line, including fascia boards, bargeboards and adjoining timber details. The apparent task may be a short soft section, a failed board end or a longer run with coating damage. Eaves linings and gutters meet this timber but are not automatically part of the same repair. The aim is to describe which component is affected and whether safe access and removal are practical before defining replacement.

What homeowners commonly notice

Look for a board edge that has softened near a gutter joint, paint that repeatedly peels, staining below a roof edge, loose fasteners or a bargeboard end that has split. Note if water is overflowing, dripping behind the gutter or tracking along an eave. Photograph the roof edge from a safe position on the ground; do not climb to inspect it for an online enquiry. The visible face alone may not show the condition behind the gutter.

How the scope is assessed

A useful scope considers the board length, profile, access height, fixings and what must be temporarily removed to reach damaged material. Gutter brackets and roof-edge fixings may affect which sections need to be released. Replacement should account for where water drains and how the new piece meets sound timber. Coating preparation and painting may be separate tasks. If water still reaches the area from roofing or guttering, correction of that source should be planned alongside the timber repair.

Where this repair may stop

A roofing or guttering trade may need to correct an active water source before repaired timber is finished. Electrical cables near an eave and unknown older lining materials need their own qualified assessment. A soft fascia can signal concealed damage behind the roof edge that ground photos cannot rule out. Safe access equipment may affect whether the job can be quoted from the initial description. Structural roof members and controlled work must follow the appropriate practitioner pathway.

Canberra conditions to mention

Canberra’s hot sun and cold periods can wear exposed paint at roof edges, while stormwater can concentrate near gutters and valleys. A board facing prevailing weather may deteriorate differently from a sheltered side of the same house. Tell us which elevation is affected and whether the gutter has overflowed. On an older home, the fascia profile and existing lining material may influence both matching and the checks needed before disturbing it.

Prepare your enquiry

Send a wide ground-level photo of the whole roof edge and a closer image of the damaged span if it can be taken safely. Estimate the length, number of storeys and access below, including garden beds, sloping ground or fixed structures. Mention any known gutter leak or overflow, the Canberra suburb and whether you want painting discussed. No climb or dismantling is needed to prepare the enquiry. You can attach photos to the enquiry; further material can be shared later if requested.
