What the work can include

Timber stair and handrail enquiries may involve a tread, riser, step edge, handrail section or a balustrade component. These parts are used for support and safe movement, so the surrounding fixings and structure matter alongside the visible timber. Describe whether the stair is internal or external, how many steps are affected and whether the item moves during normal use. A local damaged tread is not the same scope as a loose flight or altered stair layout.

What homeowners commonly notice

Record a loose tread, a change in level, an open joint, a handrail that shifts under light contact, decay on outdoor steps or a baluster with a failed fixing. Show the whole flight and landing as well as the damaged component. If an element seems unsafe, avoid using it and do not test it with weight for a photo. Images can help identify the location but cannot verify the strength or compliance of the stair assembly.

How the scope is assessed

The affected piece should be considered with its supports, fixings and connection to adjacent steps or rails. Tread support and handrail anchorage are separate questions: replacing a worn tread surface does not establish that its stringer or blocking is sound, while a rail fixing depends on the wall, newel or post behind it. Access below a tread and behind a rail may be limited by linings. Material, profile, slip resistance, finish and existing dimensions may influence the options. The agreed scope must separate a local timber repair from a larger stair or balustrade assessment, and identify any making good or coating work.

Where this repair may stop

Treads, handrails and balustrades include safety-critical components and can be subject to building requirements. If a rail moves, a step deflects or a guard is missing, avoid relying on that part until it is assessed. Structural changes or compliance-controlled work must follow the appropriate ACT practitioner and approval pathway before acceptance. A loose rail should not be assumed fixable by adding screws to an unknown substrate. We cannot certify a staircase from photos or promise that a cosmetic repair makes it compliant. Other trades may be needed if water or wall damage caused the fault.

Canberra conditions to mention

External timber steps in Canberra can experience wet winters, strong sun and rapid temperature changes; sheltered indoor stairs have different finish and wear concerns. Water at a post base or poorly drained landing can repeat the same damage after a local replacement. Tell us whether the stair is outside, covered or near a wet area. Older stair profiles may also need careful matching if only one component is changed.

Prepare your enquiry

Send wide photographs of the full stair or handrail and close views of the affected piece and its fixings, taken from a safe position. State the number of steps, approximate height, whether the stair is the main access route, any movement, your suburb and access constraints. Include existing reports or approval information if you have them. You can attach photos to the enquiry; further material can be shared later if requested.
