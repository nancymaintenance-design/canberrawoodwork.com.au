# Real project photo request for Canberra service pages

Please supply photographs of **actual, completed Ellis Services Group work** only. The website has no case-study module until a real job, its facts and publication permission have been checked. One well documented case per service is more useful than a gallery of unrelated images. The types below are requests for suitable examples, not claims that any job has been completed.

For **every case type below**, please provide:

- The actual job type and work performed; Canberra suburb or broad district **only if publishable**; date or season if known; material or timber species where useful; and any other trade or practitioner involved. State what was found only if it was observed and recorded on that job.
- A **before** wide view showing the item in context and a close view of the issue; a **during** view showing the exposed condition or fitting process only when it was safe and permitted; and an **after** wide view and matching close view from roughly the same position. Photograph the same repair through each stage where possible. Supply original, unfiltered files and identify any missing stage instead of recreating it.
- The **permission status**: client/property-owner approval to photograph and publish the images and publishable location detail; permission from any identifiable person; and any address, number plate, child, document or private interior detail that must be removed. Mark permission as **pending**, **granted with limits**, or **granted**, and provide the written record. Do not send an image for publication while permission is pending.
- Source images at **2400 × 1600 px minimum (3:2)** for landscape service use, **2400 × 1350 px minimum (16:9)** for a wide contextual or hero crop, **1800 × 1350 px minimum (4:3)** for a detail crop, and, if available, **1600 × 2000 px minimum (4:5)** for an optional vertical crop. JPG or WebP is suitable. Leave enough room around the subject for each crop; do not upscale a small image to meet a dimension.

Keep the work area safe while photographing. Do not climb, lean on suspect timber, open unknown materials, enter unsafe spaces or load a loose stair, deck or structural member for a picture. A photo can document visible work but cannot certify hidden condition, strength, compliance or approval.
